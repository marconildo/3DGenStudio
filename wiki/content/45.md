# MoCap

Film someone doing something, and put that motion on your rigged mesh. The **MoCap** tab (in the Mesh Editor's Auto Rig side panel, beside **[Skeleton](33.md)**, **[Animations](34.md)** and **[Kimodo](43.md)**) sends a video to **MoCapAnything V2**, which watches the subject and returns the motion as an animation for *your* skeleton.

The three motion tabs differ only in where the motion comes from. **Animations** picks a clip from a fixed library of pre-animated characters. **[Kimodo](43.md)** invents one from a sentence. **MoCap** copies one off a video.

What makes this tab different downstream is that there is **no bone mapping step**. The other two produce motion on *their* skeleton, which then has to be matched to yours. MoCapAnything is conditioned on your rig before it ever sees the video, so the result comes back on your mesh's own bone names — the mapping is the identity, computed rather than authored. That is what the **Prepare** step buys.

> MoCap runs on the **video-to-motion service**, configured in **Settings → Mesh Tools → Video to Motion**. It needs an **NVIDIA GPU**. The one-time install downloads roughly **3 GB** (PyTorch, Blender-as-a-module and the checkpoint). In the desktop app it is installed from Settings and started for you; elsewhere, start it from `thirdparty/mocapanything/run_server`.

> Preparing a rig needs **Blender**, which comes with the service as a Python module (`bpy`). There is no separate Blender install to do.

> This tab only works on a **rigged** mesh. Run **[Auto Rig](32.md)** first, or load a mesh that already has a skeleton.

## Workflow

1. Click **Prepare rig** and wait a few minutes. This is per mesh, not per video, and the result is cached.
2. Choose a **video** of one person doing the motion you want.
3. Set **Length to capture (seconds)**. The panel shows what it will cost in VRAM.
4. Click **Capture motion** and wait.
5. The clip lands in the **Animations** grid at the bottom of the tab. **Click it** to play it on your mesh.
6. **Tick** it and click **Save mesh with 1 animation** to embed it and save the mesh as a new version.

## 1 · Prepare this rig

Before any video can drive your mesh, the service has to learn the mesh: its skeleton topology, joint-name embeddings, a reference pose and a rendered view of the character. That is the **bake**, and it is what lets the model return motion already expressed in your bone names.

It takes a **few minutes** and uses Blender. The progress bar names each stage — exporting, extracting mesh and weights, deriving the facing direction, building reference poses, encoding the skeleton, rendering and embedding the reference view.

The result is **cached per rig**, so you wait once per mesh, however many videos you then capture. Opening the tab checks whether this exact mesh is already prepared and shows **Rig prepared — N bones** if it is.

> **After editing bones, prepare again.** The bake is keyed on the *skeleton*, so renaming, moving or deleting a bone in the **[Skeleton](33.md)** tab makes the cached bake describe a rig you no longer have. The panel warns you — *"The mesh changed since it was prepared"* — and **Prepare again** replaces it, deleting the superseded bake rather than leaving a few hundred MB orphaned on disk.

## 2 · Video

Accepted formats: **MP4, MOV, AVI, MKV**.

What the model expects:

- **One subject**, whole body in frame.
- **A single shot** with a steady camera. A cut mid-clip reads as the body teleporting.
- Only the **beginning** of a longer video is used — trim beforehand if the motion you want starts later.

### Length to capture

The model runs **one forward pass over the whole clip** — there is no chunking — so every extra second of capture is more VRAM, and length is the only budget knob you have. Type how many seconds you want and the panel tells you what it will cost before you commit the GPU time:

> About **7.8 GB** of VRAM — 150 frames at 30 fps.

Measured figures, on a clip at 30 fps:

| Length | Frames | Peak VRAM |
| ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 2s | 60 | ~5.5 GB |
| 3s | 90 | ~6.3 GB |
| **5s** (default) | 150 | ~7.8 GB |
| 7s | 210 | ~9.1 GB |
| 10s | 300 | ~10.5 GB |

The estimate turns amber past **10 GB**, which is where a 12 GB card starts to be at risk.

**Why the panel talks about your video's frame rate.** The service caps *frames*, not seconds — at most **301** in one pass, at least **32** — and it uses every frame of the video at the rate the video was shot at. So the same "5 seconds" is 150 frames on a 30 fps phone clip and 300 frames on a 60 fps one, at twice the VRAM. To keep the estimate honest, the app **measures the frame rate of the file you picked** (it plays the opening moment silently to do it) and computes from that. You will see *"Reading this video's frame rate…"* for about a second after choosing a file.

If the rate cannot be read, the panel assumes **30 fps** and says so — at a different rate the capture then covers a different length than the field says.

The 301-frame ceiling is the model's, so how many seconds you can ask for depends on the footage: **12.5s** at 24 fps, **10s** at 30 fps, **5s** at 60 fps. Ask for more and the panel says *"Capped at 10s"* rather than letting the video be truncated silently. Ask for less than 32 frames and it says *"Raised to 1.5s"*. If the video is shorter than the length you asked for, it says that too.

## What the capture drives

Once you have a capture, a set of switches appears — **Body turn, Spine, Head & neck, Arms, Fingers, Legs, Feet**. Everything is driven by default; switch off whatever should hold still.

This exists because of a real limitation. The model regresses **every joint at once** and was trained on animal and object motion, so it has no notion of *"this limb is holding still"*:

- Film only your upper body and the legs still drift.
- Body orientation lands on the root, so the whole character can swing to place a hand.

A chain that is switched off is simply not driven and holds its rest pose. This is a **filter on the mapping, not a capture setting** — it applies to a capture you have already taken, so you can try combinations without filming or capturing again.

| Switch | Off means |
| --- | --- |
| **Body turn** | The root is not driven — the character keeps facing forward. The first thing to try when the whole body swings about. |
| **Spine** | No torso bend or twist. |
| **Head & neck** | The head holds its rest pose. |
| **Arms** | Arms hold their rest pose. |
| **Fingers** | No effect in practice — see below. |
| **Legs** | Legs hold their rest pose. Use it when the subject never moved their legs. |
| **Feet** | Feet and toes hold their rest pose. |

## Hands

**A capture never contains finger motion.** Which joints are allowed to move at all is decided when the rig is prepared, and the hands are always among the ones locked — the model has no view of them. That is why the **Fingers** switch above is a no-op.

Instead, the **Hands** section holds the fingers in a **fixed pose** for the whole clip: a curl slider per hand, plus one per thumb. It is the same control as the **[Kimodo](43.md)** tab's, and it works even with **Fingers** switched off, because posing the fingers only needs the *hand* bone to be driven.

**Axis** picks which local axis the thumb folds about. Which one a rig uses is a convention, not something that can be read off the shape — if a thumb folds the wrong way, try another axis or the flip button.

## What you get

> **Motion is captured in place.** The pose is followed, but the character does not travel across the ground: the model returns joint *rotations* only, and the root never translates. A captured walk runs on the spot.

For a game clip that is usually what you want, and it means the clip loops without the character snapping back to the origin. It also means a video of someone crossing the room gives you the walk, not the crossing.

After a capture the panel reports **Frames**, **Rate** and **Bones driven**.

There is no **Match reference rest pose** option here, unlike the other two tabs. It exists to stop a reference character's stance leaking into your mesh — but here the source *is* your mesh's skeleton, so there is no stance to reconcile.

## The captured clip

The clip appears in the **Animations** grid, numbered and named after the video file. It does **not** start playing on its own — click it to retarget and play it.

> **Each capture replaces the previous one in the grid.** A capture brings its own skeleton with it, so taking a second one clears the first from the list. Save the mesh with the clip you want before capturing again — the raw motion is also kept in the motion library (below), so nothing is lost, but the grid holds one capture at a time.

From there everything works as it does in the other tabs:

- **Edit animation** opens the frame-by-frame editor under the viewport — pose any bone on any frame, trim, loop, undo. See **[Kimodo → Editing an animation](43.md)** for the full description; it is the same dock.
- **Save mesh with 1 animation** embeds the clip, edits included, and stores the result as a **new version** of the asset. The original is untouched.
- **[Mesh export](38.md)** then carries the rig and its animations to Blender, Unity or Unreal.

### Saved motions

Every capture is written to the shared **motion library** as BVH the moment the service returns it — before retargeting, before anything else can go wrong — under the name of the video it came from. A capture costs minutes of GPU time and the raw motion is worth keeping.

> The **Select saved motions** picker lives in the **[Kimodo](43.md)** tab, and it applies a stored motion through *Kimodo's* skeleton. A MoCap capture is stored on **your rig's own bone names**, so it is not interchangeable that way — to reuse a capture on another character, prepare that rig and capture the video again.

## Troubleshooting

| Symptom | What to do |
| --- | --- |
| **Prepare rig** is disabled | The mesh has no skeleton. Run **[Auto Rig](32.md)** first. |
| *"Could not reach the video-to-motion service"* | The service is not running. Start it from **Settings → Mesh Tools → Video to Motion**, or install it there if you have not. |
| Out of memory during capture | Lower **Length to capture**. The estimate under the field is what to watch; each second at 30 fps is roughly 0.75 GB. |
| The whole body swings around | Switch off **Body turn**. Global orientation error concentrates in the root. |
| Limbs move that never moved on camera | Switch off those chains under **What the capture drives**. It re-applies to the capture you already have. |
| The character stands still while the video plays | Motion is captured **in place** — that is by design. The pose should still follow the video. |
| Fingers never move | Expected. Use the **Hands** sliders for a fixed finger pose. |
| The capture follows the wrong skeleton | The rig was edited after it was prepared. Click **Prepare again**. |
| *"The captured skeleton does not match this mesh"* | Same cause — prepare the rig again. |
| The length field says a different number of frames than you expected | The video's frame rate is not what you assumed. The panel names the rate it measured. |
